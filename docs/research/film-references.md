# Night Shift · Film references and Easter eggs

Use the prop or situation to make the joke legible on a phone. The player should understand why the cruise ship has an absurdly small escape raft even if they have never seen *Titanic*. The dated chapters are described in [Temporal Dispatch](../GDD-7-eras.md). For additional UFO film references, see [Sci-fi references](scifi-references.md).

## How to read the shortlist

**Broad** means I expect the title or image to travel well across countries and age groups. **Known** means I expect wider recognition of the film or genre than of this particular scene. **Film fans** means a well-travelled classic whose specific scene is an optional second layer. These are editorial judgments, with no audience survey behind them. Each table ranks ten choices by fit for its chapter. Reference facts come from the linked film, studio, archive or original text. The designs and lines are Night Shift proposals.

Use original assets and dialogue. A standard object such as a suitcase, coconut or thermometer offers an inexpensive nod. A recognizable vehicle body, signature piece of jewelry or staged pose can carry more risk even with labels removed. Keep brand marks, franchise names, performer likenesses, costumes, voice impressions, music and shot recreations out of the game assets. Save references to exact titles and character names for these research notes. Keep the best one-liners in both EN and FR, adapting the joke over matching English syntax. A genre joke should still land in French even when its source is an English-language film. Keep the PG-13, leaning 16 tone by aiming the innuendo and mild swearing at Bureau procedure and fictional characters. [V4 tone](../../AGENTS.md#v4-deeper-jokes-2026-11-24)

The one- to three-word snippets of ordinary language are lower-risk candidates in isolation. Iconic five- to eight-word lines are best used as prompts for new jokes here. This is an editorial choice about recognisability and distinctiveness. The U.S. Copyright Office gives no word-count rule for fair use, and short phrases can raise separate trademark or endorsement concerns. France has a parody, pastiche and caricature exception with its own conditions. Check licenses separately if a production build calls for a literal brand, film prop or proprietary look.
