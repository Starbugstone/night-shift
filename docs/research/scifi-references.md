## New work orders

The three suggested adults in each work order have two exits apiece. Each exit can use the usual entry ring and checkpoint. Put at least one readable timing commitment on each route, allow the normal lock-on interval, and carry through the deep-level sunrise clock, autopilot and difficulty checks. The suggested collectibles are optional.

### 1. Soundcheck for Earth / La Terre, un deux, un deux

**Setting and specimens.** A ridge-top radio festival with an outdoor stage, an amplifier shed and a production trailer. Collect Cam, the projectionist asleep under the stage, Inez, the broadcast engineer asleep on a road case, and Mo, the producer asleep over the cue sheet.

- **Flight.** Five rehearsal lamps light parallel strips of the apron in a fixed sequence. An amber outline previews the next hard-edged strip. A live strip sees Zib under the standard exposure fuse. Cross behind the sequence, or take the wider side lane. Put the entry rings in the dark pockets between strips. The cue works with audio muted.
- **Rooms and souvenirs.** Cam leaves via a timed stage riser or a scenery door past swinging counterweights. Inez takes the fan duct or the cable passage past a bass check that can wake the booth's other sleeper. Mo takes a roof hatch past rolling road cases or a trailer ramp past a fragile stack of mic stands. The optional pickup is a pitch pipe with the wrong key circled on its tag.
- **Tutor, EN** “They booked five notes. Accounting heard six. We’re disputing the encore.”
- **FR** « Cinq notes au devis, six à la facture. La compta conteste le rappel. »

*Close Encounters of the Third Kind* (*Rencontres du troisième type*, 1977) uses five electronic notes to open communication. The new obstacle is a lighting sequence driven by the festival's tech crew. **IP risk low** for the count and original cue. The John Williams motif, its recordings, the landing tableau and the mothership design are recognizable protected material. Use original sounds and scenery. [Sony Pictures film](https://www.sonypictures.com/movies/closeencountersofthethirdkind) · [AFI tribute with Spielberg on the music](https://www.afi.com/laa/john-williams/)

### 2. The Cabbage Cup / La Coupe du chou

**Setting and specimens.** The night before a village soup competition. Collect Lucette, the judge napping in the garden pavilion, Marcel, the cook asleep in the canteen store, and Rémi, the groundskeeper asleep in the glasshouse.

- **Flight.** An automatic frost rig sweeps low sprinkler arms across the allotments as moving solids. Mark the span with amber nozzle ticks before each sweep. A judge's cart patrols the dry service lane with a headlight cone. Zib is choosing between a timing gap through the rig and the longer road crossing.
- **Rooms and souvenirs.** The pavilion has a serving hatch through hanging prize ladles and a side door past a fragile tower of tasting bowls. The canteen offers an extractor duct with steam drafts or a loading shutter beside the stockpot. The glasshouse has a roof vent with cycling louvers and a rear door past moving seed trays. The pickup is a tiny soup flask labelled “Sample for Appeals.”
- **Loading tip, EN** “For a quieter first contact, boil the cabbage with the lid on.”
- **FR** « Premier contact discret ? Faites cuire le chou à couvert. »
- **Report, EN** “Cause of sighting: wind from a neighbouring plot. The committee requests anonymity.”
- **FR** « Cause de l’observation : un vent venu du potager voisin. Le comité souhaite rester anonyme. »

In *La Soupe aux choux* (1981), an extraterrestrial mistakes a fart contest for a signal, lands in Le Glaude's garden and is offered cabbage soup. Keep the wind joke in the report and let the soup contest carry the reference. **IP risk low** for a different village, characters and story. The named characters, recognizable costumes, soundtrack and recreated scenes would raise the risk. [Cinémathèque française film record](https://www.cinematheque.fr/film/54589.html) · [Pathé film synopsis](https://www.pathehome.com/fr/fr/film/phf-EDFOAT6IHM/la-soupe-aux-choux)

### 3. Department of Missing Things / Bureau des objets disparus

**Setting and specimens.** A desert roadside UFO fair that could fill the existing gift-shop teaser. Collect Jo, its lost-property clerk, Sam, a projectionist in the viewing dome, and Bea, a stallholder asleep in a stockroom next to unsold saucer hats.

- **Flight.** A tethered festival balloon rises and falls on a fixed cue. Its cable crosses a low shortcut only while taut. An amber winch light forecasts the lift. Rooftop camcorders watch the longer, open approach. The cable is a moving solid, while cameras use the normal fuse.
- **Rooms and souvenirs.** Jo can leave via the roof past a moving archive ladder or via a returns hatch past fragile jars of “evidence.” Sam has a catwalk exit past cycling projector shutters and a service door behind a swinging screen. Bea has a delivery hatch past rolling stock bins or a stockroom vent past wire merchandise racks. The souvenir is an evidence bag containing nothing, stamped “Contents Verified.”
- **Report, EN** “We reopened the case. It was mostly unsold bumper stickers.”
- **FR** « On a rouvert le dossier. Il contenait surtout des autocollants invendus. »

*The X-Files* makes unexplained cases the business of agents Fox Mulder and Dana Scully. The 1947 Roswell debris is a separate historical reference. The U.S. Air Force attributed it to balloon-borne Project MOGUL equipment. The fair's “evidence” is fictional. **IP risk low** for the fictional fair and rewritten quip. Copying the series' poster, title treatment, theme, agents or FBI seal would raise copyright, branding or emblem concerns. [Official series page](https://www.disneyplus.com/browse/entity-d6a7aec6-72d2-46bb-b337-2cb412df64c8) · [Air Force Roswell report](https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/)

### 4. Cargo Held for Snoring / Fret retenu pour ronflement

**Setting and specimens.** An automated freight depot on land. Collect the scale operator Tessa, the dispatch clerk Niall, and maintenance fitter Vic. The computer has classified all three as unattended cargo.

- **Flight.** Carrier gantries cross container lanes on a predictable clock. Each pushes a hard-edged safety scanner ahead of its moving crossbar, with an amber endpoint marker before a change of direction. Container gaps offer cover and break up long runs.
- **Rooms and souvenirs.** Tessa can go through a top hatch past a descending barcode head or a pallet exit past a pressure gate. Niall takes a lift shaft beside the counterweight or a returns door beyond a scanner that sees the specimen. Vic leaves by a duct with a timed fan or a loading door past a cycling cargo clamp. The souvenir is a microwavable meal labelled “Company Property.”
- **Tutor, EN** “The crew’s contract says to investigate unexplained beeping. Yours says to investigate why the scanner billed us for a pallet.”
- **FR** « Leur contrat dit de vérifier les bips suspects. Le vôtre dit d’expliquer pourquoi on nous a facturé une palette. »

In *Alien* (1979), the commercial towing ship *Nostromo* investigates a mysterious transmission under a contractual obligation. Ripley interprets it as a warning and later invokes quarantine to keep the exposed crew member outside. **IP risk low** for the labor joke and generic industrial mechanisms. Distinctive creature stages, logos, character names used in-game, props and Giger's visual designs are high-risk borrowings. Keep the lights, planks and machines in Night Shift's own style. [20th Century Studios film](https://www.20thcenturystudios.com/movies/alien) · [AFI synopsis](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/53908-Alien)

### 5. Checkout, 900 Years Late / Neuf siècles de retard

**Setting and specimens.** A medieval keep operating as a modern budget hotel. Collect Ellis, the night concierge napping over a booking tablet, Yvette, the reenactment armorer asleep among exhibits, and Alain, the porter asleep at the modern laundry hoist.

- **Flight.** A drawbridge rises in sections as a moving obstacle, splitting the direct approach across the moat. A visible amber hinge cue precedes the lift. Courtyard patrol lights make the longer path a different timing choice.
- **Rooms and souvenirs.** Ellis can use the service lift past its counterweight or the stairs past a bell that can wake the relief clerk. Yvette has a battlement hatch past rotating display armor and a courtyard portcullis past a helmet stand that crashes. Alain has a roof hatch beside a linen winch or a ramp past a rolling baggage cage. The collectible is a brass room key on a chain marked “Due Yesterday, 1122.”
- **Report, EN** “Guest checked in before the invention of checkout. The late fee stands.”
- **FR** « Le client est arrivé avant l’invention du départ. Le supplément reste dû. »

Gaumont's synopsis places the start of *Les Visiteurs* in 1122. The spell sends Godefroy and his squire Jacquouille into contemporary France. A booking error and the contrast between old hardware and hotel policy are enough for the nod. **IP risk low** for that premise and the year as a detail. Avoid character likenesses, exact catchphrases and recognizable costumes or scenes. [Gaumont film record](https://www.gaumont.com/fr/film/les-visiteurs)

### 6. Please Hold for Vega / Véga vous met en attente

**Setting and specimens.** A dry plateau with a radio array, a repair bay and a signal archive. Collect Noor, a radio astronomer, Mae, a dish technician, and Colin, an archivist who has fallen asleep indexing interference.

- **Flight.** Maintenance carts pass between the dishes in pairs, their inspection lamps pointing away from each other. The dark gap between them moves with the carts. Follow it across the lane or go around the ends of the cones. An amber track marker previews each pair's arrival. Draw the dishes' radio activity as instrument feedback on the structures.
- **Rooms and souvenirs.** Noor takes the roof ramp across the elevation hinge or goes out the service tunnel past a motor shaft. Mae chooses a grease bay shutter or a lift hatch past a pulley block. Colin has an archive return chute with an updraft or a cable hatch beyond a swinging spool. The souvenir is a folded printout with 2, 3, 5 and 7 hand-labelled “Hold music.”
- **Report, EN** “We transmitted the prime numbers. They sent back a satisfaction survey. We’re holding out for intelligent life.”
- **FR** « On a transmis les nombres premiers. Ils ont renvoyé un questionnaire de satisfaction. On attend toujours une réponse intelligente. »

In *Contact* (1997), the signal associated with Vega is detected at the Very Large Array and carries sequences of prime numbers. This gives the Bureau a joking communication protocol separate from the *Close Encounters* cue. **IP risk low** for an original observatory and a list of mathematical facts. Keep the film's signal audio, machine design and characters out of the assets. [SETI Institute on the VLA scenes](https://www.seti.org/news/contact-movie/) · [James V. Hart and Michael Goldenberg screenplay](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/contact-1997.pdf)

## Rights notes

Risk ratings apply to the drafts here and are editorial triage. Keep title and character names in research notes, write fresh lines for the game, and create original art, sound and characters. Short quotations, repeated motifs, and combined costume, dialogue and visual cues can carry identifiable expression. The U.S. Copyright Office gives no automatic safe number of words or notes. France's Article L122-5 has separate provisions for short quotations and for parody, pastiche and caricature. Assess the specific use before relying on an exception. Review literal quotes and recognizable branding, particularly in level names, icons, key art and marketing, where confusing players about affiliation is a separate trademark concern. [U.S. Copyright Office on fair use](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html) · [French Intellectual Property Code, Article L122-5](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048603495) · [USPTO on likelihood of confusion](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion)

## For Fable's level ideas

Use the five anchors below. EN / FR estimates reflect likely recognition of the specific reference, with FR focused on France. **Keep** means the proposed original treatment has low editorial risk. **Rework** means keep the premise and use the suggested change. **Skip** means the particular quote, design or identifiable reenactment carries more risk than it brings to the joke. ### 2. Overtime · skyscraper
