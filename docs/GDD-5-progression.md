# Night Shift progression

Keep eight work orders. Make Maple Court the first full shift, teach its rules in a short exercise at Spin City, and introduce each later signature on the approach or in the specimen's starting room. Connect the work orders as a route through Grimsby and its surrounding districts. A delivery opens the next order. Stars reward replay.

## What players encounter today

L1 prompts drag, release to hover, lock on and beam movement on an undelivered save. Later shifts rely on memos, route labels, HUD feedback and hazard tells. A hazard label can help someone choose a route. The player still needs a place to see what the hazard does and try the response.

**First exposure** in the following inventory refers to the first work order where a mechanic exists. Deep shifts let the player choose specimens and entries in any order. A listed approach or branch can therefore be bypassed, and several mechanics can be a player's first encounter in the same room.

### Rooms and local rules

All routes use a beam with lag and speed-sensitive impacts. Letting go holds the beam. Limbs can swing past the beam, snag on a lip, and collide with walls, supports or furniture. In deep rooms, an amber beam shows strain on a snag. Ease back, settle and continue. Rotation controls can help with clearance. A captured souvenir is optional and must come through the exit. The exit checks the specimen's chest and a released grab. L6 also checks the readings.

L1–L2 allow a fully awakened specimen to recover after flailing and grabbing. Waking costs **Still asleep**. On L3 and later, wake fills more readily, decays more slowly and ends the attempt at full. A second sleeper hears impacts and other noise with distance falloff. Their own full meter ends the attempt. An active laser trips immediately. Cameras use a separate 1 s exposure rule, with grace and decay. A creak comes from moving the body too fast through a marked zone and does not require a collision.

### Scoring, shop and dispatch

- L1 and L2 score **Delivered**, **Still asleep** and **Night owl**. The par is 42 s and 48 s respectively. A flight sighting restarts the approach. It does not directly revoke Still asleep.
- L3–L7 score **Delivered all 3**, **Ghost** (zero sightings and failed abductions) and **Night owl** (finished before par within the sunrise budget). L8 scores **Quota met** (both troop quotas and Echo before the 100 s run out), **Ghost** (no full flight sighting) and **Night owl** (at least 25 s left). Delivery and stars grant Glorbs, with higher deep-shift pay. Rank tracks lifetime stars. The Beam Dampener reduces swing, Hush Drive lengthens the flight fuse and reduces Wake gain, and the Snooze Ray calms the specimen and second sleepers by 40 once per attempt with a double tap. These purchases are optional.
- Current unlock logic gives L1 and L3–L7 at the start, L2 after Doyle, and L8 after the cruise. A fresh player can bypass both tutorials on most of the campaign. Star goals and Glorbs therefore offer an economy, with little control over the order in which mechanics arrive. See [progress.ts](../src/levels/progress.ts) and [the Work Orders view](../src/ui/workorders.ts).

## Where the game jumps ahead

1. **L2 to L3 changes the cost of a mistake before it teaches the new rules.** A one-specimen tutorial win becomes a three-building shift with routing, a shared clock and a different reward for a clean run. Hard wake, secondary noise and alarms can all end the same attempt. Recovering a snag quickly in Larry's room can work against the player at Dave's stairs. Teach a safe recovery and flight respawn and room retry before testing the full shift.
2. **The tutorials can be completed by avoiding the threats that matter later.** A quiet path can avoid both L1 detectors. One L2 route skips the fan and the other skips the cart. Deep flight can then combine a stun with detection, or one detector can pick up where another leaves off. A player may never have needed to cool a partial fuse, time a full hover, wait for trailing feet to clear a gate, or distinguish a moving solid from a detector.
3. **L3 turn, carry, wait and commit can all be required under a penalty.** The same rules drive porch arming and lock-on at Gary's drive, a flap in Barb's draft and a laser crossing with the whole body. The UI should say what to do before an encounter, then let the player see the consequences at low cost. On a failure it should identify a usable alternative, such as a different ring, without restarting the shift.
4. **Each signature asks for another skill.** L5 changes where it is safe to hold still and how fast to drag. L6 asks for a body joint to hold, then may kick it. L7 teaches both breaking a photographer's frame and anticipating a room-wide force. L8 reverses the usual assumption that a detector's centre is bad. These are good changes of pace. Give each an isolated first decision before adding its usual overlaps.
5. **The map order has no readable handoff.** Campaign order in a menu and delivery goals in a memo explain what to do tonight. A recurring landmark, a shift report that tees up the next order, and a recommended dispatch path explain why the next shift follows this one.

## Progression design

### The first full shift

Keep both tutorial entries playable and label one recommendation per work order using verified clearance and delivery, then put the hazard in the second route's label. Add a short fuse exercise at Doyle's pickup verge: place the safe waiting spot just outside the sweep, mark the lateral exit and let one brief edge clip clear on its own. Use Larry's street to teach a wire gap while the van is clear and show active neon somewhere the player can steer out. Put the slow fan or moving cart in view before committing to its crossing.

After Larry's report, offer **Field Check** as the default continuation for a fresh save. Use the edge of Maple Court and Dave's building assets in a 45–75 s practice scenario. The exercise runs without the paid shift clock. The player can skip it and reopen it from L3's memo. Each piece has one action:

1. Fly past a single porch sensor, release in a safe ring and see the lock finish. Show the arming trigger and wider light pool from the quiet side. One short contact teaches escaping and letting the fuse clear.
2. Lift a practice specimen past one door lip, observe the wake meter, stop the beam and let the body settle. Place a mock sleeper well away from the opening. A scripted practice clatter makes both meters visible and lets the player see how loudness falls off.
3. Show a practice retry from the current room and a checkpoint marker after delivery. The end of the exercise displays one filled specimen slot and a short explanation of the shared sunrise clock. Preview the live timer and the three slots on dispatch into L3.

Start with placements, the existing prompt and the recommended entries. Add the scenario only after the approach and prompt pass so the same pieces are usable in it. Reuse a short simulation fixture with a known spawn, one target, one practice exit and a local reset. Keep practice completion outside scored runs, with no stars or pay. Give practice tells the sim's real extents and timing. A practice hazard that cannot fail an attempt should visibly read as a Bureau training device.

In normal L3, recommend Dave first if his route passes the casual route check. Spawn toward a side approach with one porch lesson and a full lock-on interval. Use Dave's bedroom clearance as the first hard-wake crossing. The patio saw lower modeled peak wake than the roof, so start there and check creak exposure with fresh players. Put Gary's early timing problem in view from a generous waiting pocket on both paths, then use his garage door for camera and laser sequencing. Keep the other targets selectable. Teach a signature in a shared approach or in every entry that requires it. A recommendation alone can be missed.
