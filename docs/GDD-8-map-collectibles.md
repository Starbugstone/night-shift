# Night Shift · Town pickups

Each town map from L2 onward can have one optional, hand-placed pickup. It can be an object or a cameo character, and it gives Vrell something extra to write about. **Rule:** every map pickup must be hinted in that level's shift memo (and if the memo already hints at a concrete object or character on the map, that is the pickup). **L1 (Doyle Farm) has no map pickup** so the opening shift stays focused on flying. Spin City (L2) is the first map with one. The existing room souvenirs and L8's roaming ape quotas keep their own rules and report entries.

## Finding and collecting

- Fly close enough to spot the prop and its four-point mint glint. Its silhouette should read at phone size against the ground. The glint stays lit under reduced motion. L2 uses this world marker. Maps with a minimap also show one mint diamond, separate from the round specimen marks, with no bearing arrow or additional HUD chip.
- Slow to **110 logical px/s or less** and hover within **48 logical px** of the prop for **one second**. This uses the same `LockOn` thresholds and drain as a roaming ape. A segmented mint arc fills around the pickup and drains at twice the fill speed when the hover breaks. The active specimen ring takes priority anywhere the two catchments overlap.
- The prop and its marker disappear when the lock completes. A mint pulse, the existing pickup chime and a short confirmation toast acknowledge the pickup. The toast stays visible without animation in reduced-motion mode. Control stays live throughout, so you can move off immediately.
- The detour costs travel time and the hover itself. Detectors, scrutiny and wire/obstacle stun still apply. A sighting interrupts an unfinished pickup, and a stunned saucer cannot build hover progress. A finished pickup stays aboard through a sighting or a return from a room during the same shift. It gives no spawn point, shield, fuse reset, Glorbs or star.

## Time, stars and placement

Tutorial Night owl uses the normal scored flight and abduction time, including the detour on that flight. Deep shifts keep their shared sunrise clock running during the detour and hover, including the L8 countdown. Ghost uses the ordinary spotting and failed-abduction rules for the shift. If the optional detour costs time or makes the player get spotted, the usual consequences apply. A pickup never unlocks a route or gets counted toward a delivery or troop quota.

Place the pickup off the main approach and outside entry rings, their labels and L8's moving trails. Let the player see the prop from a place where they can back out. Start with roughly 160 logical px between entry and pickup centres, then check the actual pills and moving hazard geometry. On deep maps, keep the diamond about 12 minimap px from neighbouring objective markers. A van cone or lighthouse sweep can make the detour interesting if there's a readable safe approach and a full second to hover. Where sweeps overlap, check a safe wait point, the hover window and an exit with the actual fuse rules. Keep the whole hover pocket clear of wires, bump areas and the edge of the map.

At 375×690 CSS px, verify the sparkle against each ground type, the lock arc against the hull and the report with long French names and room items. Avoid roof edges and other decorative glints that can obscure the prop. Test a clean detour and the normal skip route with no upgrades. Run the difficulty evaluator for the relevant map and keep the naive beeline caught at every tested start time. For L8, also confirm there is a plausible detour with 25 seconds left at delivery before adding the pickup.

## Art, idle animation and name tag (built 2026-11-25)

The first pickups were 40 px sketches drawn from above, and nobody could tell what they were. Each pickup now has its own art, drawn three-quarter from the south like a standing prop, about the saucer's size (84–130 logical px on its long side, sizes in `PICKUP_SIZE`, `src/flight/pickup-rig.ts`).

- **Keys.** `flight/pickup_<id>` (the `MapCollectibleId`) is the whole picture standing still. The trophy room shows the same key. The art carries no text: code writes the flyer's `LOST` / `PERDU` header and the crate's `O™`.
- **People move.** A pickup that is a person (`PERSON_RIGS`: the astronomer and Krix) is cut into layers by `scripts/art-pickups.mjs` and idles: blinks, glances sideways, yawns, and has two beats of its own (the astronomer sips from the thermos cup and fiddles with the dish; Krix hugs the toaster and the toast pops). A beat starts every 2–4 s and blinks come more often, on a timeline seeded per pickup (`figurePose`, `src/flight/pickup-pose.ts`), so two people never move in step. Reduced motion keeps only a slow blink. Objects stand still. A new person pickup needs a rig entry, its layer keys and a drawing in the script.
- **One class.** `PickupFigure` (`src/flight/views/collectible.ts`) builds any pickup at any scale: layers when it is a person and they all loaded, else the whole art, else the primitive sketch (`pickupFallback`), scaled to the same footprint.
- **Name tag.** Within 260 px of the saucer an uncollected pickup shows a small paper tag with its name under it (in the flight's speech-bubble layer). It fades in over the last 40 px, gives way to the lock ring while the hover fills, and route pills keep clear of it. Under reduced motion it appears without the fade.
- **Lock ring.** The segmented mint ring goes round the whole prop (radius 56–72 px). The hover catchment is unchanged (48 px, one second).
- **Placement.** The art's box stays off the lanes of every road, off buildings and off entry rings (`tests/flight-pickup-figure.test.ts`). No pickup had to move: the L2 flyer sits against the laundromat wall with its edge on the kerb, and the L6 trolley is drawn low (84 px) to fit its grass island.

## Filing and replay

Keep one optional pickup ID on each level's progress in `night-shift-save-v2`. A report that logs a new pickup records it even on a failed delivery or at sunrise. Quitting before filing clears the current shift's cargo. Older saves load with no pickup recorded (and retired preprod IDs such as `seti_dish` are safely dropped on load), and a later missed pickup leaves the earlier record intact. A new shift has the pickup available again. A future Bureau cabinet could use the recorded IDs to show finds by level. The report is the only collection screen in this release.
